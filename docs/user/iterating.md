# Iterating — your second application, refining, and earlier work

> **Purpose:** what to do after your first tailored résumé: start the next application, ask
> for changes to a finished résumé, and find, track and reopen applications you've already
> made.
> **Audience:** `user` — anyone who has finished one application with Sartor. No technical
> knowledge assumed.
> **Type:** how-to
> **Authoritative for:** starting a new application; what carries over between applications;
> the refinement flow after a résumé is generated; Candidate memory; finding and tracking
> earlier applications on the Pipeline tab. The first application, step by step, is in the
> [walkthrough](walkthrough.md).

Sartor gets more useful with each application. Your career corpus and the answers you've
given stay with you, so later applications start from more than the first one did.

---

## Starting your next application

**What it does:** clears the current run and takes you back to Step 1, ready for a new job
description.

**How:** on the **Tailor** tab, click **↻ Start new tailoring** next to the wizard rail. A
note confirms "Started a new tailoring run." Paste the new job description and click
**Analyze**; that creates the new application.

**What to expect:** your corpus and your earlier applications are untouched. Only the
in-progress run is cleared: the pasted job description, the analysis, your answers and your
Compose choices for *that* run. If the run was worth keeping, it's already saved, and you can
reopen it from **Pipeline** (see [below](#finding-earlier-applications)).

### What carries over between applications

| Carries over | Where it lives | What it means for the next application |
|---|---|---|
| Your **career corpus**: roles, bullets, titles, skills, education, summary variants | **Career corpus** tab | Every application draws from it. Improve a bullet there and every *later* application gets the better version. |
| Your answers to Clarify's questions | **Candidate memory** tab | Sartor can reuse facts you confirmed for an earlier job, so you aren't asked for the same number twice. |
| Your templates and settings | **Résumé templates** tab, **⚙ Settings** | Unchanged. |
| Your earlier applications | **Pipeline** tab | Kept, with every version you generated. |

What does **not** carry over: the choices you make in Compose (pins, excludes, edits). Those
belong to one application, because what fits one job rarely fits the next. To make a change
permanent, make it on the **Career corpus** tab instead. Edits there apply to the next
application, not to one already in progress.

## Refining a résumé

After a résumé is generated, Step 6 shows a **Document refinement** area with two ways to
improve it.

### Refine: ask for one change

**What it does:** turns a plain-language request into **one targeted change**, a reworded
bullet or summary, that you review before it's used.

**Why you'd use it:** for requests about wording and emphasis, such as "make the tone more
formal" or "emphasize cloud experience". The box says what it's for: "Scope: document content
only — tone, emphasis, keyword placement, structure."

**How:**
1. Type your request in the box (up to 600 characters) and click **Refine**.
2. If your request looks like it would change facts rather than wording, a
   **"Heads up — this may change facts"** message appears. Choose **Proceed anyway** or
   **Cancel**. It's a warning; it never blocks you.
3. Sartor works on it ("Finding the one thing to change") and takes you **back to Step 3 —
   Compose**.

**What to expect in Compose:**
- If it found a change: a banner, **"One targeted change is ready for your review"**, shows
  what you asked, the current text crossed out, the proposed text, and why. Click **Accept** to
  use it or **Retire** to discard it.
- If it couldn't draft one: a banner, **"Adjust your content here, then regenerate"**, invites
  you to make the change yourself in Compose. Click **Got it** to dismiss it.

Either way, finish with **Save and continue to Template →**, then **Generate documents** in
Step 5. That produces the new version with no further AI cost.

Each Refine uses AI twice: a quick check of your request, then the draft of the change. If
you'd rather change the wording yourself, **✎ Edit before downloading** in Step 6 costs
nothing.

### Get follow-up questions

**What it does:** asks new clarifying questions aimed at the weaknesses of the current draft,
building on the answers you've already given.

**How:** click **Get follow-up questions**. (This needs at least one generated résumé.) A
**Follow-up clarification** section appears with the questions. Answer the ones that apply,
then click **Submit answers and regenerate**, or **Skip and close**. If there's nothing worth
asking, it says so: "No follow-up questions surfaced — the current draft looks healthy on the
measured signals."

**What to expect:** your answers are saved to Candidate memory, where later applications can
use them.

**If you edited the preview first:** asking for a refinement or follow-up questions while you
have unsaved edits in **✎ Edit before downloading** opens **"Your edits aren't saved yet"**.
Choose **Use edits as baseline** to keep them, **Discard edits**, or **Cancel**.

## Candidate memory

**What it is:** every clarifying question you've answered, across all your applications, in
one place. Open the **Candidate memory** tab.

**Why it matters:** these answers are facts you've confirmed about your own experience. Sartor
draws on them when it drafts for later jobs, and you can turn a good one into a permanent
bullet.

**What you can do:**
- **Search** questions and answers ("Search question or answer…"), and filter by kind of
  question.
- Tick **Outcome-rich only** to see answers with measurable results, the strongest bullet
  material.
- Click **Promote to bullet** on an answer, choose which role it belongs to, and click
  **Promote**. It's added to your corpus for you to review. Promoted answers are hidden unless
  you tick **Show promoted**.

**What you can't do (yet):** edit or delete an answer here. Memory belongs to one user;
nothing in it is shared with other users.

## Finding earlier applications

Every application you've started is on the **Pipeline** tab, grouped into five columns by
status: **Draft**, **No response yet**, **Got interview**, **Rejected**, and **Withdrawn**.
Each card shows the person, the job title, the company, and when it last changed. Click
**Refresh** to update it.

If you tailor résumés for several people, Pipeline shows everyone's applications together;
see [Coaching several people](coaching.md).

### Opening an application

Click a card to open its details:
- the job title and company (both editable), and the job description;
- each version you generated ("Iteration 1", "Iteration 2", …), with its résumé, any cover
  letter, and an **ATS** (applicant tracking system) result: Sartor's own check that screening software can read the file
  back (see [Templates](templates.md#the-ats-rules-every-template-should-follow));
- a **Notes** box for your own notes about the application;
- **Resume in wizard**, which reopens the application where you left off, with your analysis,
  answers and Compose choices restored, and no new AI cost. The button appears only when there
  is something to resume.

### Tracking what happened

Status changes are yours to record; Sartor never submits anything for you.
- After you download a résumé, Step 6 asks **"Submitted this application?"**. Click **Mark
  submitted** to move it from Draft to **No response yet**. You can also do this from the
  application's details.
- When you hear back, open the application and click **Got Interview**, **Got Rejection**, or
  **Withdrew**. These are final.

### Retiring an application

**Retire** in an application's details hides it from Pipeline; its history is kept. **Right
now you can only undo this before closing that window**: the **Restore** button appears in its
place, but once you close the details, a retired application no longer appears anywhere in the
app. The confirmation message mentions a "Show retired" option, which Pipeline doesn't have
yet. Retire only applications you're sure you won't need to reopen. (Tracked as a
[known issue](../dev/work/items/0131-retired-application-unrecoverable-from-pipeline.md).)
